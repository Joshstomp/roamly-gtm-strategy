# GTM Strategy · Roamly Groups

> Module 1 · Deliverable 1 · V1 draft informed by the Discover Framework, skeptical-CMO critique, and strategic pushback. Validate assumptions and edit after class feedback.

## 1. Discover Framework

### Align on the Goal
- **Core business problem:** Roamly risks losing revenue from customers already trying to book experiences together. They coordinate with separate accounts, manual split payments, and off-platform scheduling; group sessions show high drop-off.
- **What success looks like:** Booking as a group becomes nearly as straightforward as booking solo; fewer group-intent sessions abandon checkout; existing customers generate incremental completed-booking revenue. Organizers return, invitees discover Roamly, and hosts receive more predictable group demand.

### Do the Research
- **Signals of a real opportunity:** Customer Support hears about group coordination workarounds; group sessions show elevated drop-off; customers ask to book together; Roamly reports repeat booking at 3× the industry average; 12,000+ local experts across 40 cities provide potential supply. These signals suggest demand but do not prove its incremental revenue impact.
- **Top 5 possible segments:** (1) Friend-group organizers of birthdays, reunions, and bachelor/bachelorette trips; (2) multi-generational families; (3) small-company offsite organizers; (4) wedding/destination-event planners; (5) clubs and affinity communities.
- **How to size the opportunity:** Analyze group-intent sessions, drop-off stages, typical group sizes, completed booking values, and net revenue contribution. Triangulate Support tickets, host capacity and inquiries, user interviews, and external category data. Estimate *recoverable incremental bookings* and contribution after payment, support, refund and cancellation costs; avoid treating all abandoned sessions or all invitees as incremental demand.
- **Evidence still needed:** Baseline group conversion and abandonment by step, dominant abandonment reasons, host capacity in pilot markets, payment-success rate, net revenue economics, and whether invites convert new customers.

### Build a Working Team
- **Cross-functional stakeholders:** Product and UX Research; Data/Analytics; Payments/Finance; Host/Supply Operations; Customer Support/CX; Product Marketing; Growth/Lifecycle; Community/Social; Legal/Trust & Safety. Finance and Payments should validate split-payment economics and failure modes early.

### Form Your Hypothesis
- **Most valuable segment to target first, and why:** Existing Roamly friend-group organizers showing group intent. They have acute pain, can initiate a self-serve purchase, and can bring companions into the booking flow. Focus on high-intent behavior rather than demographic labels alone.
- **Core value proposition:** Plan together. Book together. Pay your share. Organizers can coordinate an experience, share plans, invite friends, and collect individual payments without fronting the entire bill or chasing payments.

## 2. GTM motion
- **Chosen motion:** Product-led growth (PLG) as the primary motion, supported by a small, measurable community-led (CLG) pilot.
- **Why:** The initial buyer is an existing consumer, the purchase is self-serve, and organizers can distribute an invitation link. In-product entry points and lifecycle recovery reach people close to purchasing. Invitations must first improve transaction completion; acquisition virality is a hypothesis to test, not a guaranteed result.
- **Time to value:** Minutes to create and share a group plan (hypothesis); the decisive value moment is a completed group booking.
- **Deprioritize:** Sales-led corporate offsites and broad awareness spending in V1.

## 3. Primary goal
**Expand:** Increase *incremental net completed-booking revenue* from existing Roamly customers who organize group experiences.

- **Primary business measure:** Incremental net group-booking revenue vs. a credible baseline or control group, net of refunds, payment and support costs.
- **Leading indicators:** Group booking conversion; organizer activation; invite acceptance; payment completion; time to completed booking.
- **Guardrails:** Cancellations, refunds, payment failures, host fulfillment, Support load, and cannibalization of bookings that would otherwise occur.
- **Target setting:** Establish a pre-launch baseline before committing to numerical lifts.

## 4. The assumption I'm pushing back on
I'm pushing back on the idea that this is merely an unproven product hypothesis. Roamly already has behavioral evidence: customers coordinate across accounts, manually split costs, and contact Support about group booking. This is stronger than hypothetical demand.

However, that evidence does **not** prove coordination friction is the primary reason groups abandon checkout, nor how much lost revenue the new flow can recover. Rather than delay the strategy or claim the outcome is certain, launch a focused, measurable pilot with a comparison group, instrument the funnel, and expand only if the data supports incremental revenue and acceptable operational risk.

## 5. V1 GTM strategy one-pager

### Launch thesis
Roamly Groups can unlock incremental revenue by helping existing travelers complete group experiences that are currently difficult to coordinate and pay for.

### Beachhead ICP
Existing friend-group organizers on Roamly, particularly those planning birthdays, reunions and bachelor/bachelorette trips, who already show group-booking intent.

### Customer promise
**Plan together. Book together. Pay your share.** Coordinate plans, invite friends, and split payment in one self-serve experience.

### Motion and recommended channels
**Primary: PLG. Supporting experiment: CLG.**
1. **In-product discovery:** Surface Groups in relevant experience search and booking moments.
2. **Activation:** Take organizers from experience selection to shared invite to confirmed group commitments.
3. **Invitation loop:** Reduce friction for invitees to join and pay; track acquisition impact separately.
4. **Lifecycle recovery:** Test targeted re-engagement for high-intent users who abandoned group sessions.
5. **Limited community pilot:** Publish occasion-specific content in relevant travel communities, with conversion tracking.

### Goal and suggested success measures
- **Goal:** Expand incremental net completed-booking revenue from the existing customer base.
- **Primary outcome:** Incremental net group-booking revenue relative to a credible baseline/control.
- **Leading metric:** Group-intent session to completed group booking conversion.
- **Supporting indicators:** Organizer activation, invite acceptance and payment completion.
- **Guardrails:** Refunds, cancellations, payment failures, host experience and support costs.
- **Numeric targets:** To be set after baseline measurement, not invented.

### Launch scope and investment
**Provisional Tier 2 focused launch**, pending Reach / Impact / Risk scoring. Pilot with existing high-intent users in markets with adequate group-capable host supply. Do not initially pursue corporate sales motions, multiple new segments, or major paid media.

### Validation plan
1. Baseline group-intent drop-off, economics and reasons for abandonment.
2. Interview organizers and review Support/behavioral evidence.
3. Pilot the product experience with a defensible comparison cohort.
4. Track completed bookings, incremental net revenue and guardrails.
5. Scale only if the product demonstrably recovers profitable bookings.

### Strategic bet and tradeoff
**Bet:** Removing coordination and payment friction for existing Roamly organizers creates more incremental completed bookings than launching broad awareness campaigns.

**Tradeoff:** Prioritize the booking funnel and existing demand; defer corporate offsites, broad paid acquisition and expansion into secondary segments until the initial economics are validated.

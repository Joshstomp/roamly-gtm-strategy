# ROAMLY GROUPS
**V1 Go-to-Market Strategy | Internal Working Draft | Module 1**

## PLAN TOGETHER. BOOK TOGETHER. PAY YOUR SHARE.

**Our strategic thesis:** Recover incremental group-booking revenue by removing coordination and payment friction for existing Roamly customers already trying to book experiences together.

## 1. Strategic Choices

| Strategic choice | Our approach |
|---|---|
| **Target customer** | Existing friend-group organizers planning birthdays, reunions, and milestone trips |
| **Customer problem** | Separate accounts, manual payments, and disconnected planning create booking friction |
| **Customer promise** | Coordinate experiences, invite friends, share itineraries, and split payments in one place |
| **GTM motion** | Product-led growth (PLG) first, with targeted community-led support |
| **Primary goal** | **Expand:** Increase incremental net completed-booking revenue from existing customers |

## 2. Four Launch Channels

**01 · In-product discovery**  
Introduce Groups during relevant experience searches and checkout.

**02 · Lifecycle recovery**  
Re-engage customers who previously showed group-booking intent or abandoned checkout.

**03 · Invite-driven activation**  
Enable organizers to invite friends into shared plans and individual payment flows.

**04 · Community amplification**  
Test occasion-based messaging in relevant travel and social communities. Treat this as a supporting experiment, not a substitute for in-product activation.

## 3. How We Measure Success

- **Primary conversion metric:** Group-intent session-to-completed-booking conversion versus a comparable control group.
- **Business impact metric:** Incremental net group-booking revenue.
- **Leading indicators:** Organizer activation, invite acceptance, payment completion, group-booking abandonment, and new customers attributable to invitations.
- **Guardrails:** Refunds, cancellations, payment failures, host fulfillment, and support costs.

*Numerical targets will be set after baseline measurement; suggested improvements are not yet validated outcomes.*

## 4. Launch Plan

**Start focused.** Pilot with existing friend-group organizers in markets where hosts can accommodate groups.

**Validate.** Instrument the booking funnel and compare the pilot with a suitable control. Determine whether reducing coordination and payment friction increases completed bookings and net revenue, rather than merely shifting existing bookings into a new flow.

**Scale deliberately.** Expand only after demonstrating measurable improvement without unacceptable operational costs.

## 5. The Assumption I'm Pushing Back On

The group-booking opportunity is not merely speculative. Existing Roamly users already coordinate through separate accounts, split costs manually, and contact Support about booking together. These are meaningful signals of demand.

However, this does **not** prove that coordination friction is the primary cause of abandoned bookings or establish how much revenue fixing it would recover. The right response is a focused, instrumented launch—not indefinite delay or an unsupported promise of uplift.

## 6. The Strategic Bet

**Fix the booking experience before investing in broad awareness.**

We believe reducing group coordination and payment friction for existing customers will generate more incremental completed bookings than immediately funding broad acquisition campaigns.

**Trade-off:** Prioritize existing demand, self-service adoption, and measurable conversion improvements. Defer corporate offsites, enterprise sales, and large-scale paid acquisition until the initial launch demonstrates value.

**Open question:** How much group-booking abandonment is actually recoverable through a simpler coordination and payment experience?
